In this article, I want to talk about the things that, in my opinion, can make a career in IT feel tough and _suck_. Just think of this as a young person's rant while going through a _quarter-life crisis_ and hoping to become successful quickly.
*btw* so this doesn't go all over the place, the scope of this discussion is *staying relevant*, okay? By staying relevant, I mean that our careers can keep _advancing_. Not just stay stuck in the same place.

## _Too Much to Learn_
Sure, it's no secret that you have to keep learning if you want to succeed, but does a new _framework_ really have to appear every month? Within a year, it's already become the standard. *GraphQL*, for example, only had its _stable release_ in 2018. By 2020, plenty of jobs already required you to know it. In the _mobile_ space, there's Flutter too. Or at the _library_ level, Facebook suddenly releases *Recoil*. _it's just too much_. 
And that's only in one field, never mind if you want to become full-stack. Haha, that's why I think twice these days when someone asks me to go full-stack. There's already no end to what you need to learn in just one field.

## _Too Easy to Learn If You Have Plenty of Time_
So, if the issue earlier was _too much_,now it's _too easy_. But precisely because it's _too easy_, very young talent starts popping up incredibly quickly, ready to threaten their seniors' *very* young talent starts popping up, ready to threaten their seniors' _job security_. Hahaha. The thing is, everything is available now—on YouTube, in articles—it's all easy to find if you look. Especially when you're still a student and don't have work responsibilities yet. The unlucky ones are those who are already working: they have to deal with _deadlines_, maybe deal with other problems in life, and still stay relevant by keeping up with knowledge that may be easy to learn but comes in massive amounts.

In the end, once these young talents have mastered the new technologies/standards, they're the ones who get hired. From a company's perspective, that makes perfect sense. _age doesn't matter_. 

## _The Community Isn't Always Healthy_
Yeah, the IT community isn't always _healthy_, especially its online communities. You often see someone ask a simple question only to get _bullied_. If you don't want to answer, you can just skip the post, right? Another slightly annoying thing is when people mock someone for asking for a tutorial in Indonesian. If there isn't one, just don't give them one. Simple. 

Why should learning IT require learning English? If our IT community really is _healthy_, newcomers ideally shouldn't even need to learn English because the content available in Indonesian should already be good enough.

# _How We Can Grow_
So then, how can _we as a community grow_?

## _Make Learning Easy_
By creating more Indonesian-language content that's free, if possible, and high quality. This would make it easier to spread knowledge and support learning.

## _As a Company, Make Room for Learning_
I've heard that American companies like Facebook have a system where employees can spend one day a week contributing to open source. This would really help _software engineers_. And it's far more meaningful than expensive training that only lasts a few days. Diving into open source and contributing directly gives you real experience. It can even be motivating because succeeding at it could support your future career. 

## _Let's Be a Healthy Community_
I don't think we need to bully each other. We are all beginners at some point. If you're annoyed, just skip it. There will surely still be people willing to answer.
